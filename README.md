HeightCalculator.run is a free online height comparison and height calculator website. It helps you compare people, celebrities, fictional characters, animals, landmarks, and everyday objects on the same visual scale. You can also convert height units, check reference percentiles, compare growth measurements, estimate height from a calibrated photo, and explore height-related questions with dedicated calculators.

All public height tools are free to use. You do not need to log in, create an account, register, subscribe, or pay to use the comparison tools and calculators. Open a page, enter the measurements you know, and read the result immediately.

## Start with the homepage height comparison tool

The [free 3D height comparison tool](https://www.heightcalculator.run/) turns a numerical height difference into a visual comparison. Add your own height, another person’s height, a celebrity, a character, an animal, a building, or a familiar object. The tool places the entries on one ground line and uses one scale so the relative difference is easy to understand.

You can use the homepage to:

- Compare two people or build a larger group comparison.
- Enter heights in centimetres or feet and inches.
- Search a reference library of celebrities, fictional characters, animals, landmarks, vehicles, and everyday objects.
- Rotate the human figures to view the comparison from the front, side, or back.
- Change the baseline person when you want to read every difference relative to a particular entry.
- Zoom or fit the chart so a couple, family, team, or character lineup is easy to read.
- Download a PNG result card with the names, measurements, comparison result, and HeightCalculator.run branding.
- Share an editable comparison link or QR code with someone who needs to view or adjust the lineup.

The chart represents the heights you enter or select. It uses a common scale, while the illustrated body shapes, hairstyles, and poses remain visual examples rather than portraits of real people. For a reliable personal comparison, use measured heights taken under similar conditions.

### Height Calculator: family reference and current growth context

The [Height Calculator](https://www.heightcalculator.run/height-calculator) answers two related but separate questions. First, it calculates a mid-parental family reference from the father’s and mother’s heights, with the traditional sex adjustment shown clearly. Second, when you add age and current height, it can place that measurement beside a CDC height reference.

This page is useful when you want to:

- See a transparent family-height reference based on parental measurements.
- Understand the difference between a family reference and a current growth-chart comparison.
- Review a child’s current measurement in an age- and sex-specific context.
- Send a selected height into the free 3D comparison scene.

The family formula is a population reference, not a promise of a final adult height. A single percentile also cannot describe a child’s complete growth pattern. Dated measurements, measurement technique, family history, development, and health context all matter when a professional assessment is needed.

### Height & Weight Percentile Calculator: ages 2–20

The [Height & Weight Percentile Calculator](https://www.heightcalculator.run/height-weight-percentile-calculator) is designed for children and teenagers from age 2 through 20. Enter age, reference sex, height, weight, or the available measurements to compare them with the CDC 2000 age-specific reference.

It can help you read:

- Height-for-age reference position.
- Weight-for-age reference position.
- BMI-for-age context when the required measurements are available.
- The difference between a percentile reference and a medical diagnosis.

The result describes where a measurement sits within a reference population. It does not determine whether a child is healthy, predict adult height, or replace advice from a qualified healthcare professional.

### Ideal Body Weight Calculator: adult screening context

The [Ideal Body Weight Calculator](https://www.heightcalculator.run/ideal-body-weight-calculator) is for adults and presents two kinds of information together: an adult BMI screening interval and four historical ideal-weight equations. Comparing formulas helps you see why different calculators can return different values for the same height.

Use it for general reference, a weight-equation comparison, or a starting point for understanding the relationship between height and a screening range. “Ideal weight” is not a universal personal target. Age, body composition, medical history, pregnancy, athletic training, and other factors are outside a height-only equation.

### Average Height by Country: understand the reference cohort

The [Average Height by Country](https://www.heightcalculator.run/average-height-by-country) page lets you search country and territory means, compare male and female entries, inspect rankings, and send selected values into the 3D comparison tool.

The displayed values describe a modelled adult-height reference for a defined birth cohort: people born in 1996 and measured at age 18 in 2014. A country mean is a statistical summary. It does not describe every person from that country, and it cannot be converted directly into an individual percentile or a prediction about a specific person.

### Height Difference Calculator: numbers and visual scale

The [Height Difference Calculator](https://www.heightcalculator.run/height-difference-calculator) gives the numerical answer to “how much taller?” or “how much shorter?” Enter two heights to see:

- The signed difference, which keeps the direction of the comparison.
- The absolute difference, which shows the size of the gap.
- The ratio between the two heights.
- The percentage relationship.
- A link to view the same pair in the 3D comparison scene.

This is a useful page for couples, friends, family members, sports comparisons, character research, and any situation where a centimetre or inch difference needs a clear explanation.

### Image to Height: estimate dimensions from a known reference

The [Image to Height tool](https://www.heightcalculator.run/image-to-height) estimates a person’s or object’s dimension from a photo that includes a reference with a known measurement. You mark the reference and the subject in the image, then read the proportional estimate.

For the most useful result, the subject and the reference should be close to the same plane and the image should have limited perspective distortion. Camera angle, lens distortion, posture, hidden feet, uneven ground, and an uncertain reference can change the estimate. The tool is best treated as a visual estimate, not a replacement for a direct measurement. Photos are handled in the browser, and no account is required to try the tool.

### Height Converter: centimetres, metres, inches, and feet

The [Height Converter](https://www.heightcalculator.run/height-converter) makes common height formats easy to compare. It supports centimetres, metres, total inches, decimal feet, and feet-and-inches notation.

Examples of useful conversions include:

- Converting a metric measurement into feet and inches for a form or conversation.
- Converting feet and inches into centimetres for a chart or comparison.
- Checking total inches when a calculation needs one numeric unit.
- Distinguishing decimal feet from feet-and-inches notation.

Displayed feet-and-inches values are rounded for readability. Keep the original measurement when precision matters, especially if a rounded result is converted back again.

### Growth Velocity Calculator: change between dated measurements

The [Growth Velocity Calculator](https://www.heightcalculator.run/growth-velocity-calculator) uses two height measurements and their dates to show the observed change and an annualized equivalent, usually expressed in centimetres per year.

It is useful for keeping a simple measurement record, comparing two points in time, or understanding why a raw centimetre change and a yearly rate answer different questions. Measure in a consistent way, record the dates, and avoid treating one interval as a complete description of growth. Growth is not perfectly constant, and a calculator cannot diagnose the reason for a change.

### Dunk Calculator: standing reach and required jump

The [Dunk Calculator](https://www.heightcalculator.run/dunk-calculator) starts with measured standing reach, a rim or target height, and any desired clearance above the target. It estimates the vertical jump required to reach that height.

This is a planning calculation for basketball and other reach-based goals. It does not guarantee that someone can dunk, because approach speed, take-off technique, hand control, timing, strength, and safety all affect performance. Measure standing reach and target height in the same units before comparing results.

### Baby Length Percentile: birth to 24 months

The [Baby Length Percentile Calculator](https://www.heightcalculator.run/baby-length-percentile-calculator) uses recumbent length references for babies from birth through 24 months. It is designed for a baby measured lying down, which is a different measurement from standing height.

Use the matching age, reference sex, and recumbent length. The WHO reference helps place a measurement within a population distribution; it does not diagnose a baby’s health or explain a change in growth by itself. Repeated measurements taken with a consistent method provide more context than one isolated value.

## How to get a more useful result

1. Choose the tool that matches the question you are asking.
2. Measure people barefoot on a level surface when possible, with a consistent posture and method.
3. Keep the unit clear: centimetres, metres, inches, decimal feet, or feet-and-inches.
4. For growth calculations, record the date and repeat measurements with the same technique.
5. For a photo estimate, include a known reference close to the subject and avoid strong perspective angles.
6. Read the method and limitation notes on the tool page before interpreting a reference value.
7. Use the 3D chart for perspective and the numerical result for the exact value entered.

## Reference information and responsible interpretation

HeightCalculator.run explains the source or method used on each calculator page. Some pages use CDC references for children and teenagers, the WHO reference for infant recumbent length, historical equations for adult weight context, or a defined country-height cohort for statistical comparisons.

Reference data describe populations and formulas. They do not diagnose a condition, determine a person’s worth, guarantee a final adult height, or establish a universal “correct” body size. If you are concerned about a child’s growth, an unexpected change, symptoms, or development, discuss the dated measurements with a qualified healthcare professional.

The 3D models are illustrations. They show the selected height on a common scale and do not recreate an individual’s face, body proportions, posture, shoes, hair, or clothing. The result is most useful when you separate the entered measurement from the appearance of the model.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is HeightCalculator.run free?

Yes. The public height comparison and calculator tools are free to use.

### Do I need to log in or register?

No. You can open the public pages, enter measurements, compare results, and use the available sharing and download functions without logging in or registering.

### Do I need to pay or enter payment details?

No payment is required to use the public tools, and the calculators do not require a paid plan to show a result.

### Can I compare more than two heights?

Yes. The homepage comparison can be used for pairs and group lineups. Add the entries you want, choose a baseline when helpful, and fit the chart to the screen.

### Does the 3D comparison predict adult height?

No. It visualizes the heights you enter or select. Use the separate Height Calculator and its reference notes when you need a family-height context, and do not treat a single reference value as a prediction.

### Are the country averages personal height predictions?

No. Country values are statistical means for a defined reference cohort. They are useful for population comparison, not for predicting an individual’s height or percentile.

### Is a percentile a medical diagnosis?

No. A percentile locates a measurement within a reference population. Growth assessment also considers measurement history, development, family history, symptoms, and other context.

### Can I use the baby calculator with standing height?

The baby page is for recumbent length measured lying down from birth through 24 months. Standing height and recumbent length should not be mixed without using the matching reference and method.

### How accurate is an image-based height estimate?

It is limited by the reference measurement, camera angle, perspective, lens distortion, posture, and whether the subject and reference are in the same plane. Treat it as an estimate and use a direct measurement when accuracy matters.

### Can I save or share my comparison?

Yes. You can download a PNG result card or share an editable comparison link. A shared link contains the comparison data, so share it only with people who should see those details.
